## Long Story Short

Couples plan weddings across several tools like WhatsApp, Google Sheets, a notes app, PDFs, emails, a seating website, and a budget spreadsheet, and more. None of them talk to each other. The result is a stressful process that turns a joyful event into a project management nightmare. why not make it an easy and fun run?

## Core Flow

<CoreFlowGrid>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Setup" title="Onboarding">A quick five-step setup. Add the names, the date, the venue, the budget, and invite your partner. Takes a few minutes and your whole workspace is ready to go.</CoreFlowStep>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Overview" title="Dashboard">Your home screen. Days left, what you've spent so far, what's due this week, who's RSVP'd, and where each vendor is in the booking process.</CoreFlowStep>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Planning" title="Timeline">A simple timeline of what to do when. Tasks grouped by how far away the wedding is - a year out, six months out, the week of - so nothing sneaks up on you.</CoreFlowStep>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Coordination" title="Vendor workspace">Each vendor has their own page with the contract, payments, contact info, notes, and what you still need to do with them. No more digging through email.</CoreFlowStep>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Logistics" title="Guests and seating">Track who's coming, what they're eating, and where they're sitting. Drag people around the seating chart - it'll warn you if you accidentally sit two guests who shouldn't be together.</CoreFlowStep>
</CoreFlowGrid>

## Visual Direction

Calm, warm, and deliberately unhurried. The palette draws from champagne and cream — luxe without being loud. EB Garamond brings an editorial quality to headings; Hanken Grotesk keeps the UI crisp and legible. The goal was to feel like a beautifully printed invitation, not a SaaS dashboard.
