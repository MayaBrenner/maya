## Long Story Short

Relocation is a scary and confusing thing, you leave everything you had home and start a new life in a new place. Start collecting furniture for yourself? Sucks. Home Again helps you buy in bundle with zero worries or headaches

## Research

I interviewed 12 people who had relocated in the past 18 months - some moving out of a city, some moving in, some doing both at once. I focused on how they handled the furniture and objects problem, not just which apps they used.

<Metrics>
  <Metric value="12" label="Users interviewed" sublabel="Recent movers in and out of Tel Aviv, Berlin, and NYC" />
  <Metric value="10 / 12" label="Used FB Marketplace" sublabel="Described it as 'stressful,' 'ghosty,' or 'a lottery'" />
  <Metric value="8 / 12" label="Paid more for reliability" sublabel="Would pay 20–30% above market if handoff was guaranteed" />
</Metrics>

Two distinct user types emerged clearly from the interviews:

**The Leavers** - people leaving their apartment for another city or country. Everything is unclear and emotional, there's a lot of bureaucracy to deal with, so they prefer to offload the "headaches" - furniture, their apartment, a car - rather than manage them from afar.

**The Arrivals** - moving in, not familiar with local options, need to furnish fast. Sensitive to price but more sensitive to trust. They'd rather pay a little more for someone with good reviews, and ideally take as much as possible in one trip.

## Visual Direction

I wanted the design to be sleek, uncomplicated, and fun. As this concept is based on encouragement and connection, I want the user to enjoy the experience and feel happier just logging in.

Colors and graphics should provide joy and excitement without overstimulating the user or feeling childish.

## Design Decisions

### Why not just show the address on the listing?

Because the handoff is what creates trust, not the address. Showing the exact address publicly invites bad actors and removes the natural safety mechanism of mutual confirmation. Home Again uses a staged reveal: general area visible on the listing, exact address shared only after both parties confirm a meetup. The seller is in control.

### Why seller pages instead of just listings?

Listings are items. Seller pages are people. A person leaving Tel Aviv for Berlin on March 28 is one of the most useful pieces of information a buyer can have - it tells them the urgency, the timeline, and the context behind every item that person is selling. The seller page turns that information into something scannable and actionable.

### Why 3 stacked action buttons instead of tabs?

Tabs imply equal weight. The three actions - Ask, Meet, Reserve - are a progression, not alternatives. Stacking them in order of commitment communicates that naturally. Asking is free. Meeting requires coordinating. Reserving requires money. The visual hierarchy says that before you read the labels.
