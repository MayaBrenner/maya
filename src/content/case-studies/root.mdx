## The Problem

You're at the supermarket looking at six varieties of potato, and your recipe says "potatoes." You grab the closest one. Dinner is gluey.

This isn't a lookup problem — it's a knowledge problem. The difference between a floury King Edward and a waxy Yukon Gold is the difference between a silky purée and a gummy one. That knowledge lives with chefs and grandmothers. It's almost never at the moment of decision.

<Callout type="insight">
The pivot that defined this project: instead of building a produce app for every vegetable in the store, go deep on one. A single ingredient, treated with Michelin-level seriousness. The potato sommelier — not a produce database.
</Callout>

I interviewed 12 home cooks about their potato-buying behavior. The conversations kept returning to the same gap: confidence, not information.

## Two Paths, One Chef

The app is built around a single AI persona — the Chef — but users arrive in two fundamentally different states. The design has to serve both.

<ProcessTimeline>
  <Step phase="Path 01" title="The chef route — I have a dish">
    The cook knows what they're making. They open Root, tell the Chef — by voice or text — and get a recommendation in under 30 seconds. Variety, reasoning, and a three-rule in-store checklist. Both paths converge in the kitchen and feed back into the Cellar.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Path 02" title="The scan route — I have a potato">
    The cook is standing at a market stall, potato in hand, no idea what to cook. They point their camera at it. The AI identifies the variety in 0.6 seconds and returns a ranked list of what it's best for — and what to avoid. The inverse of the chef route, same result.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Debrief" title="The feedback loop — the chef learns from you">
    After cooking, a single question: how was it? Silky, gluey, or grainy? The answer trains the chef's understanding of how this cook handles this variety. Honest feedback is the mechanic that makes the AI genuinely personal over time.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="The Cellar" title="Mastery tracking — six varieties, deep not wide">
    Every dish cooked with Root adds to the user's cellar — a record of which varieties they've mastered and which they're still learning. A 12-day streak is one mechanic only. The real win state is the day you stop needing to ask.
  </Step>
</ProcessTimeline>

## Design Decisions

### The AI has a persona — not a voice

Most AI assistants are deliberately personality-free. The Chef is the opposite — opinionated, terse, always in italics, always gold.

*"For purée, you want starch and butter. King Edward — passed through a tamis twice. Trust me."*

That's not a database answer. It's a chef's answer. The persona makes the advice feel earned rather than generated. The italic serif is doing work: it signals expertise, not neutral information retrieval.

### The scanner as the inverse flow

The scanner wasn't in the original design. It came from research — 4 of 12 interviewees described standing at a market stall not knowing what to cook with what they were holding. The chef flow assumes you know the dish. The scanner assumes you know the potato.

Both flows converge at the variety detail and debrief screens. The architecture is a fork-and-merge, not two separate apps.

### No ratings, no reviews — only the chef

Every rating system eventually produces the same thing: a race to the top. Root has no user ratings. The Chef's score is the only signal — and it's reasoned, not aggregated.

*"The chef reasons in plain language. No black box."* The pairing screen shows the top three matches with a one-sentence reason for each. The score bar below each card is a visual shorthand, not the primary information.

### The Michelin aesthetic as a design argument

The visual language is a deliberate argument: potatoes are as serious as wine. Ink on cream paper. Gold sparingly. Italic serif for the chef's voice. Montserrat all-caps for metadata. No color photography — potato SVG illustrations instead.

This isn't decoration. It says: *this knowledge is worth treating with care.*

## Outcome

Root is a speculative project demonstrating:

- **AI-first product design** — a conversational AI with a strong persona, transparent reasoning, and a feedback loop that makes it genuinely personal over time
- **Single-product depth** — the deliberate narrowing to one ingredient is a product decision. It's what makes the expertise feel credible rather than broad and thin
- **Dual-path architecture** — designing for two entry states (dish-first vs. ingredient-first) that share the same AI and converge at the same screens
- **Visual identity as argument** — the Michelin aesthetic isn't branding. It's a claim that this knowledge is worth treating seriously
