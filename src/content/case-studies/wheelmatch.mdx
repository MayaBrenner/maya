## The Problem

Buying a car is one of the most stressful large purchases people make. Hundreds of models, specs that mean nothing without context, and salespeople with incentives that don't align with the buyer.

First-time and infrequent buyers walk into dealerships completely unprepared — or they spend weeks lost in comparison websites that present more data than clarity.

<Callout type="insight">
The core problem is not a lack of information — it's a lack of a trusted guide. People don't need more specs. They need someone (or something) to ask the right questions and translate the answers into a recommendation they can believe in.
</Callout>

## Research

I mapped the existing car-buying journey across three user types: first-time buyers, upgrading families, and people replacing an old car. Key pain points:

- **Information overload** — comparison sites show 40+ attributes per car with no prioritization
- **Trust gap** — dealerships are structurally misaligned with buyer interests
- **Spec blindness** — "200hp" and "7-speed transmission" mean nothing to most buyers
- **Post-decision anxiety** — buyers doubt their choice for months after purchase

The insight that guided the design: confidence is the product. The user doesn't just need a car match — they need to feel sure about it.

## The Expert Handoff

The most novel feature of WheelMatch is the "Get Expert Eyes" step. After the AI surfaces 3-5 matched cars, the user can send their shortlist to a vetted human car advisor who reviews the picks within 24 hours.

This solves the trust gap that no AI alone can close. The expert can confirm the AI's reasoning, flag potential issues, ask follow-up questions the AI missed, or recommend a specific configuration.

<Callout type="insight">
The expert handoff is a human-in-the-loop UX pattern. The AI does the heavy lifting of narrowing from hundreds of options to five. The human does the trust-building work that converts uncertainty into confidence.
</Callout>

## Design Decisions

<ProcessTimeline>
  <Step phase="Onboarding" title="The question flow">
    15 conversational questions in plain language: budget, usage, family size, city vs. highway, must-haves, color preferences, new vs. used. Progress shown as a simple bar, not numbered steps.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Results" title="Match cards">
    3-5 car cards each showing: match percentage, 3 plain-language reasons why, and key specs translated into human terms.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Expert" title="Expert handoff">
    One-tap to send shortlist to an expert. Expert profile card shows credentials. Status states: waiting, expert reviewing, response received.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Export" title="Dealership prep">
    A generated "visit brief" — what to ask, what to avoid, the fair price range — so the user walks in prepared.
  </Step>
</ProcessTimeline>

## Visual Direction

Clean and confident. Navy, white, and a single electric lime accent. The AI "thinking" animation — a subtle pulsing glow, not a spinning loader — is the signature design moment that signals intelligence without pretense.

Expert profile cards use warm photography and minimal credentials. The contrast with the AI's clean interface signals "real person" immediately.

## Outcome

This is a speculative project demonstrating AI-first product design: conversational UI, AI output display with transparent reasoning, a human expert handoff flow, comparison table design, and trust-building patterns across a high-stakes decision journey.
