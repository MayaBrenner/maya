## The Problem

Buying a car is one of the highest-stakes, lowest-confidence decisions most people make. Hundreds of models, specs that mean nothing without context, salespeople with misaligned incentives, and no trusted guide anywhere in the process.

First-time buyers and infrequent buyers walk in completely unprepared — or they spend weeks in comparison sites that present more data than clarity.

<Callout type="insight">
The core problem isn't a lack of information — it's a lack of a trusted guide. People don't need more specs. They need someone (or something) to ask the right questions and translate the answers into a recommendation they can believe in. Confidence is the product.
</Callout>

## Research

I mapped the car-buying journey across three user types. Not to design for all three, but to understand which moments of the journey were broken for everyone.

**The four pain points that showed up across all user types:**

- **Information overload** — comparison sites show everything, prioritize nothing
- **Trust gap** — dealerships are structurally misaligned with buyer interests
- **Spec blindness** — "200hp" and "7-speed DSG" mean nothing to most buyers
- **Post-decision anxiety** — doubt persists for months after the purchase

## The Expert Handoff

The most novel feature of WheelMatch — and the one that anchors the whole product — is the human expert review step.

After the AI surfaces 3–5 matched cars, the user can send their shortlist to a vetted car advisor who reviews the picks within 24 hours.

<Callout type="insight">
This is a human-in-the-loop UX pattern. The AI does the heavy lifting: narrowing from 200 options to 5. The human does the trust-building work that converts uncertainty into confidence. Neither can do the other's job. The design has to make both halves feel seamless.
</Callout>

This solves the trust gap no AI recommendation alone can close. The expert confirms the AI's reasoning, flags issues the algorithm missed, or asks follow-up questions the onboarding didn't cover.

## Core Flow

<ProcessTimeline>
  <Step phase="Onboarding" title="15 conversational questions">
    Budget, usage pattern, family size, city vs. highway, new vs. used, must-haves, color preferences. Plain language, no spec jargon. Progress shown as a simple filling bar.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Results" title="Match cards with transparent reasoning">
    3–5 car cards, each showing: match percentage, 3 plain-language reasons why, and key specs translated into human terms ('fits 5 adults comfortably' not '2,850mm wheelbase').
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Expert" title="One-tap handoff to a vetted advisor">
    The user sends their shortlist with a tap. Expert profile shows credentials and response time. Status states: waiting → reviewing → response received.
  </Step>
  <Step phase="Export" title="Dealership prep brief">
    A generated visit brief: what to ask, what to avoid, the fair price range, which configurations matter. The user walks in knowing more than the salesperson expects.
  </Step>
</ProcessTimeline>

## Design Decisions

### The AI thinking animation

The AI's "thinking" state is a subtle pulsing glow — not a spinning loader, not a progress bar. The glow signals intelligence and computation without pretending the answer is loading linearly.

This was a deliberate departure from standard loading patterns. A spinner says "wait." A glow says "working on something."

### Transparent AI reasoning

Every match card shows the top 3 reasons why this car was recommended — in plain language. Not a match score alone.

This was the most important trust-building decision in the product. A number you don't understand is worse than no number. The reasoning turns a black box into a conversation.

## Outcome

WheelMatch is a speculative project demonstrating:

- **AI-first product design** — conversational onboarding, AI output with transparent reasoning, confidence-building patterns across a high-stakes decision
- **Human-in-the-loop UX** — designing the handoff between AI and human expert as a first-class product experience, not an afterthought
- **Complex flow design** — a 4-stage progressive funnel (questions → results → expert → export) where each stage must earn the next
