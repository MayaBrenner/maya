## Long story short

Buying a car is one of the most stressful decisions for a person who doesn't understand anything about it. I created a site that shows everything you really need to know (and not the big words on all the websites) without missing out on the confidence that someone who understands will confirm that you've chosen the right car for you.

## Research

I mapped the car-buying journey across three user types. Not to design for all three, but to see which moments of the buying experience were hard for everyone.

## Core Flow

<CoreFlowGrid>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Onboarding" title="Answer the questions">Budget, usage pattern, family size, city vs. highway, new vs. used, must-haves, color preferences. Plain language, no spec jargon.</CoreFlowStep>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Matching" title="Get your cars">3–5 car cards matched to your lifestyle, each with plain-language reasons why — not just a score.</CoreFlowStep>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Expert review" title="A real person confirms">Send your shortlist with a tap. A vetted car advisor reviews your picks within 24 hours and confirms the match.</CoreFlowStep>
  <CoreFlowStep phase="Dealership" title="Walk in prepared">A generated brief tells you what to ask, what to avoid, and the fair price range. You know more than the salesperson expects.</CoreFlowStep>
</CoreFlowGrid>
